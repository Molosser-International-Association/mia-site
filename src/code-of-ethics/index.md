## 1. Purpose and scope

The Molosser International Association (MIA) exists to keep honest, verifiable records of molosser breeds. This code sets the standard of conduct for everyone who uses the registry, and explains how MIA handles complaints.

The code applies to anyone who registers a dog or litter, applies for registration, transfers a dog, or attends an MIA appraisal. MIA has no paid membership; registration privileges are what MIA can grant, limit or withdraw.

## 2. Definitions

- **Participant:** anyone this code applies to under section 1.
- **Registration privileges:** the right to register dogs and litters, transfer dogs, order pedigrees and attend appraisals.
- **Ethics committee:** the people MIA appoints to review complaints, at least [3] people.
- **Complainant:** the person who files a complaint.
- **Respondent:** the participant the complaint is about.
- **Days:** calendar days.

## 3. Responsibilities of breeders and owners

Participants must:

1. **Keep records accurate.** Submit only true information about a dog's identity, parentage, ownership and dates. Correct any error as soon as it is found.
2. **Identify every dog.** Microchip every registered dog and keep a DNA profile on file with an MIA-accepted lab.
3. **Health test breeding stock.** Complete MIA's required health tests for the breed before breeding: [required tests per breed].
4. **Breed responsibly.** Breed only dogs that are registered with MIA and meet its rules, including a minimum breeding age of [X months] for dams and [X months] for sires, no more than [N] litters per dam in [period], and [color breeding rules].
5. **Provide humane care.** Give every dog proper food, water, shelter, veterinary care, exercise and socialization.
6. **Cooperate with MIA.** Allow MIA to verify records, including parentage DNA testing, when asked.

## 4. Selling and transferring dogs

1. **Tell buyers the truth.** State a dog's registration status (Pedigreed, Graded or Foundation) and share its health test results before sale.
2. **Use a written agreement.** Every sale or placement of a registered dog has a written contract signed by both sides.
3. **Transfer promptly.** Start the MIA ownership transfer within [30] days of the sale.
4. **Never sell registration you don't have.** Do not promise MIA papers for a dog or litter that is not registered or eligible.
5. **No brokers or auctions.** Do not sell registered dogs through [pet stores, brokers or auctions].

## 5. Prohibited conduct

Any of the following can lead to penalties under section 8.

**Cruelty**

- Neglect, abuse or inhumane treatment of any dog, in breeding, training, housing or showing
- Dog fighting or any blood sport, or training dogs for them

**Dishonesty**

- Giving false information on any MIA application, form or appraisal
- Forging, altering or misusing an MIA certificate, registration slip or signature
- Registering a dog as another dog, or a mixed-breed dog as purebred
- Misstating a dog's parentage, registration status or health results
- Using another person's registration or kennel prefix without permission

**Conduct toward MIA**

- Threatening, harassing or abusing MIA appraisers, staff or other participants
- Trying to influence an appraisal or complaint through gifts, pressure or deception
- Breaking a penalty MIA has already imposed

## 6. Reporting a concern

Anyone can report suspected cruelty. Complaints about other breaches can be filed by any participant or by a buyer of an MIA-registered dog.

Send complaints in writing to [{{ site.email.ethics }}](mailto:{{ site.email.ethics }}). Include:

- your name and contact details (anonymous reports are reviewed only for cruelty)
- the respondent's name and the dogs involved, with registration numbers if known
- what happened, when and where
- any evidence: documents, photos, messages, vet records

Filing fee: [none / $X, refunded if the complaint is upheld]. Suspected cruelty should also be reported to local animal control or law enforcement.

## 7. Investigation and decisions

1. **Screening.** The ethics committee decides within [14] days whether the complaint falls under this code and has enough evidence to review. If not, it is closed and the complainant is told why.
2. **Notice.** The respondent receives the complaint and evidence in writing.
3. **Response.** The respondent has [21] days to reply in writing and submit evidence. No reply means the committee decides on what it has.
4. **Review.** The committee may request records, DNA tests or statements, and may hold a call or meeting with either side.
5. **Decision.** A majority of the committee decides, in writing, with reasons. Both sides are told the outcome within [60] days of the complaint, or given a reason for the delay.
6. **Interim action.** Where a dog's welfare or the registry's records are at serious risk, MIA may suspend registration privileges while the case is open.

## 8. Penalties

The committee chooses a penalty that fits the breach, from least to most serious:

| Penalty | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Written warning | Kept on the participant's record |
| Correction | Records fixed at the participant's cost, for example a DNA parentage test |
| Fine | [$ amount or range] |
| Suspension | No registrations, transfers or appraisals for a set period |
| Registration action | Affected registrations marked under review, changed or cancelled |
| Permanent ban | Registration privileges withdrawn for good; no new registrations from that kennel |

Cruelty and forged records are referred to the authorities in every case. Suspensions and bans may be shown on the public registry record of the dogs involved.

## 9. Appeals

Either side may appeal once, in writing, within [30] days of the decision. An appeal must point to new evidence or to a step in section 7 that was not followed. It is decided by [MIA's directors / people not on the original committee]. The appeal decision is final.

## 10. Conflicts of interest

Anyone involved in a complaint or appraisal must step aside when they, their family or their kennel has a stake in the outcome, including:

- dogs they bred, own or co-own, or dogs from their bloodlines
- a buyer, seller or business partner of theirs
- a complaint about a relative or a competitor

This applies to MIA's founders and officials too. Where too few people are left to decide, MIA brings in an independent reviewer: [who].

## 11. Confidentiality and changes

Complaints and investigations are kept confidential. Only the people deciding the case see the details. Outcomes may be published only as described in section 8.

MIA may update this code. Changes are posted on the website with their effective date and apply only to conduct after that date.
